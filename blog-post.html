Engagement

Why every professional event in Norway now needs an event engagement platform

By Ingunn Dahl-Engh, CEO Tappin April 21, 2026 8 min read
Featured story · Tappin Stories

Event engagement in Norway has fundamentally shifted, and the shift is permanent. We've been backstage at hundreds of physical events, hybrid events, and digital events through the last decade, and the same pattern keeps showing up, no matter the format or the audience.

People are not showing up for the slides. They are showing up for something content alone cannot provide.

For years, event organizers built programs around content: a strong keynote, a panel with familiar names, a few breakouts, and a hope that audiences would be sufficiently impressed to come back next year. That formula is breaking. And the platforms used to run those events haven't caught up, which is why every professional event in Norway now needs a dedicated event engagement platform.

The connection imperative

Attendees now arrive expecting something content libraries and YouTube can't deliver: professional connection. The information is already free. The expertise is already on a podcast. The thing that pulls people out of their office and onto a train is the chance to meet someone whose work they admire, or whose problem they share.

This is especially true in Norway. Norwegian professionals are direct, time-conscious, and selective. They won't travel three hours to hear a talk that could have been a recording. They'll travel three hours for a conversation. Twenty meaningful conversations. Or one introduction they couldn't get any other way.

"A coffee break is not a networking strategy. Engagement cannot be left to chance, it must be designed and operated like any other system in the business."

What "engagement" actually means in 2026

Engagement has become an overloaded term. So let's be precise. We're not talking about reactions per post. We're talking about structured infrastructure that turns passive attendance into active participation. In practice, that means four capabilities, working together:

1. Pre-event attendee discovery

Attendees see who else is coming, search by goal and industry, and build a list of people to meet, before the doors open. The value of the event becomes visible weeks in advance, which dramatically lifts the show-rate. This is what a modern branded event app does in the pre-event window.

2. Structured in-event networking

Smart matchmaking, scheduled 1:1 meetings, topic-based lounges, and an attendee app that puts the right person in the right place at the right time. No more standing alone holding a coffee.

3. Live in-session engagement

Polls, Q&A with upvoting, anonymous reactions, word clouds, and live chat, the heart of any event engagement platform. The speaker stops broadcasting and starts conversing. The room stops nodding and starts contributing.

4. Post-event follow-up that closes the loop

The connections, the questions, the leads, captured, structured, and routed into CRM via integrations from registration through check-in to engagement. The event doesn't end when the lights come down. The data and the relationships keep working for months.

The structural shift required

Most event teams still operate on a "broadcasting" model, a stage, an audience, a feedback form afterward. That model produces predictable, increasingly poor results: declining show-rates, NPS that doesn't move, and leadership that wonders if the budget could be better spent.

The teams getting it right are running a participatory model. They design for participation, not around it. Sessions are shorter. Networking is scheduled, not hoped for. Speakers are briefed on how to use live tools, not just slides.

And here's the part most organizations miss: this requires platform infrastructure, not just intent. You cannot wedge participatory design onto a tool stack built for broadcasting. The five-tool patchwork of registration + email + polling + networking + video has a structural ceiling on what it can deliver, because the data doesn't flow between any of them.

The Nordic context

Norwegian audiences set a high bar. They're skeptical of fluff, allergic to corporate theater, and very willing to vote with their feet. That's a feature, not a bug, it forces organizers to design events that justify the time investment.

In our research across 200+ Norwegian event leads in 2025, three signals consistently separated the high-performing events from the rest:

  • Engagement rate above 50%, measured as % of attendees actively participating in polls, Q&A, or networking
  • Pre-event app activation above 60%, attendees who open the event app at least once before arrival
  • Post-event NPS above +40, separating events worth defending from events worth quietly retiring

None of these numbers are achievable without infrastructure designed to produce them. They aren't outcomes of a great agenda, they are outcomes of a great system.

Content's new role

None of this is an argument against great content. Excellent speakers still matter. Sharp programming still matters. But content's role has shifted, from being the destination to being the starting point. The spark for the conversations that follow.

In the best events we see, the keynote isn't the climax. It's the prompt. The real value sits in the next 45 minutes, what people say to each other in the lounge, in the structured 1:1s, in the open Q&A. The platform's job is to make those 45 minutes effortless.

Where this leaves you

If you're planning a professional event in Norway in 2026 and the engagement layer is still an afterthought, the math has changed. Your audience has already adjusted their expectations. Your competitors are running events with measurable engagement rates north of 60%. Your leadership has more options for spending the same budget than they did three years ago.

The path forward isn't more content. It's connection at scale, operated by a platform that treats engagement as the product, not a tab in the dashboard.
