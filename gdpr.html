GDPR Event Platform Norway, Tappin's Compliance Information

How Tappin operates a GDPR event platform in Norway, what we do, where customer data lives, and the safeguards in place. Hosted in Norway, DPA standard with every customer, transparent on subprocessors and rights.

Last updated: 1 March 2026

Summary

Tappin AS is fully compliant with the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Norwegian Personal Data Act ("Personopplysningsloven"). We've built our platform with GDPR-first principles: data minimisation, purpose limitation, transparent processing, and clear data subject rights.

Roles & responsibilities

Our role under GDPR depends on the context:

  • Data controller, for personal data collected directly through tappin.no (e.g. contact forms, marketing subscriptions).
  • Data processor, for personal data processed on behalf of our customers (event attendee data on the Tappin platform). The customer remains the data controller in that context.

Every customer signs a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) before using the platform.

Hosting & location

All personal data processed by the Tappin platform is hosted on infrastructure located in Norway and the EU/EEA. We do not transfer personal data outside the EU/EEA unless covered by an appropriate transfer mechanism (Standard Contractual Clauses or adequacy decision).

Subprocessors and their locations are listed in the DPA. The list is reviewed quarterly. Material changes are communicated to customers with at least 30 days' notice.

GDPR principles in practice

Tappin's product and operations are built around the seven GDPR principles:

  • Lawfulness, fairness, transparency, clear privacy notices, consent mechanisms where required, and accessible information about processing.
  • Purpose limitation, data collected only for specified, legitimate purposes.
  • Data minimisation, we collect only what's required to deliver the service. Customer-controlled forms support optional fields explicitly.
  • Accuracy, data subjects can correct their data via the attendee app or by request.
  • Storage limitation, defined retention periods, automatic deletion at end of customer agreement (30-day grace period).
  • Integrity & confidentiality, TLS 1.3 in transit, AES-256 at rest, role-based access controls, audit logging.
  • Accountability, documented policies, regular DPIA reviews, internal training, and external audits.

Data subject rights

Individuals whose personal data we process can exercise the following GDPR rights:

  • Right of access (Article 15)
  • Right to rectification (Article 16)
  • Right to erasure / "right to be forgotten" (Article 17)
  • Right to restriction of processing (Article 18)
  • Right to data portability (Article 20)
  • Right to object (Article 21)
  • Rights related to automated decision-making (Article 22), Tappin does not perform automated decision-making with legal effect.

For data controlled by Tappin directly, requests can be sent to post@tappin.no. For event attendee data, requests should first go to the event organizer (the data controller).

We respond to verified requests within 30 days, in line with Article 12.

Breach notification

In the unlikely event of a personal data breach, Tappin will notify:

  • Datatilsynet (the Norwegian Data Protection Authority) within 72 hours of becoming aware, in line with Article 33.
  • Affected customers and data subjects without undue delay, where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights (Article 34).

DPO & supervisory authority

Tappin's Data Protection Officer (DPO) can be reached at post@tappin.no. Mark your email "Attention: DPO".

If you believe Tappin has not handled your personal data lawfully, you have the right to lodge a complaint with the Norwegian Data Protection Authority (Datatilsynet, datatilsynet.no).

Why Norway hosting matters for a GDPR event platform Norway customers trust

Event data is unusually sensitive. A typical Tappin customer event covers names, professional roles, dietary requirements, accessibility needs, networking preferences, session attendance, Q&A submissions and sometimes financial transactions. The Tappin GDPR event platform Norway architecture keeps that data inside Norway, not because it is a marketing claim, but because Norwegian-hosted infrastructure removes an entire class of cross-border transfer risk for our customers' procurement and legal teams.

Data hosted within Norway and the EU/EEA falls under the GDPR's strongest protections without invoking Standard Contractual Clauses, Transfer Impact Assessments, or the post-Schrems II complexity associated with US-based providers. For Norwegian customers in regulated sectors, finance, healthcare, public sector, energy, this materially shortens the procurement cycle and removes a recurring source of audit friction.

The infrastructure is operated by EU-based providers under Standard Contractual Clauses where any cross-border processing is unavoidable. Encryption keys are managed within the EEA. Access by Tappin staff is role-based, audit-logged for 12 months, and protected by mandatory multi-factor authentication. Backups are encrypted and tested with documented restore procedures.

How Tappin handles common GDPR scenarios

Three GDPR scenarios come up consistently with new Tappin customers in Norway. Understanding how each is handled tends to remove the bulk of compliance questions before they reach legal.

Right to erasure during an active event. If an attendee requests deletion in the middle of an event, the request is honoured immediately for any non-transactional data. Transactional data (payment records, attendance records required for tax or audit purposes) is preserved for the legally required minimum, then deleted. The data subject is informed of the timeline in writing.

Data subject access requests. Attendees can request a copy of all data Tappin holds on them via post@tappin.no. Verified requests are answered within 30 days with a structured export. For event organizer customers, the same workflow runs through the Tappin admin portal.

End of customer agreement. When a customer agreement ends, attendee data is retained for a 30-day grace period during which the customer can export everything they need. After that period, all customer data is permanently deleted from production systems and backups, and a certificate of deletion is issued on request.
